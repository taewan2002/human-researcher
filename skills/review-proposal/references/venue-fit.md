# Review a proposal against its publication target

Use for a target-venue assessment. Preserve review-only scope; when revision is requested, correct the proposal and recheck the affected claims, evaluation, resources, and visuals.

## Verify the actual target

- Identify venue, conference year/edition and track, or journal article type and special issue. Do not mix main-track, workshop, or prior-edition requirements.
- Inspect current official scope, calls, author instructions, and review criteria where tools permit. Record URLs or supplied excerpt locations, access dates, applicability, and uncertainty. Do not claim live verification from an offline packet.
- Keep official requirements distinct from selected published examples, third-party advice, your recommendations, and user constraints. Preserve mandatory versus recommended or optional language. Resolve conflicts by authority and applicability, not just the newest date.
- Do not infer today's deadline, page limit, mandatory experiment count, or editorial preference from memory or an older edition. If the needed sources are unavailable, mark the corresponding venue judgment unassessed and name the missing input.

## Judge the mapping, not the label

Map each consequential criterion to the proposal's intended contribution, closest and contrary work, necessary evidence, resources, and section location. Check whether the plan advances the relevant audience's question, whether the claim fits the article type, and whether the proposed evidence can support it.

A replication, measurement study, theoretical result, or informative negative finding can be appropriate where the actual scope supports it. Do not demand a new algorithm merely because the target is a conference, or extra datasets merely because it is a journal. Conversely, do not claim fit from topic overlap alone when the selected track requires a different contribution.

Check whether eventual paper requirements change the research plan. Distinguish them from the format of the current internal proposal. A short proposal need not fill a full-paper page allowance. Check a deadline's year, stage, and time zone before judging the schedule; do not invent a deadline or fixed review turnaround for rolling submissions.

## Deliver separate judgments

Report a compact mapping with source status, proposal location, gap, and next action. Distinguish:

1. **Scientific readiness:** whether the question, evidence, evaluation, and feasibility connect.
2. **Target fit:** supported within inspected scope, conditional on named evidence, mismatch, or unassessed, with reasons.
3. **Submission-format readiness:** only if the requested artifact and available rules allow checking it; not implied by a good proposal.

For five-criterion scoring, state the audience and contribution context but retain the declared anchors and weights unless the user supplies an alternative. Report venue fit separately; do not add a hidden bonus, convert it into acceptance probability, or portray a changed target as a scientific improvement. Scores across different targets, evidence access, or rubrics are not directly comparable.

Separate defects in the research plan from missing venue information or a venue mismatch. If a target cannot be supported within the available resources, recommend an evidence, scope, or target change and explain its cost; do not silently make that decision. Do not add speculative reviewer demands, hide contrary evidence, or rewrite a review-only request.
