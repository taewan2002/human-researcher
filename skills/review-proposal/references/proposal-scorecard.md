# Evidence-linked proposal scorecard

Use when asked to score or rate a proposal. This is Human Researcher's working assessment framework, not a validated measurement instrument, acceptance probability, or proof of research quality. Honor a supplied rubric or weighting scheme and disclose departures from these defaults.

## What the five criteria assess

| Criterion | Decision question | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| **Timely** | Why is this question worth investigating now? | Dated survey coverage; foundations and recent relevant work; verified follow-ups; closest and contrary findings; a supported unresolved need, changed constraint, or newly testable question. |
| **Practical** | What useful change or knowledge could this work enable, and for whom? | Concrete use or scientific need, relevant conditions, plausible value of positive and negative results, and limitations on transfer. Basic or theoretical research need not promise a product. |
| **Analytical** | Can the proposal explain its question, mechanism, assumptions, and strongest alternative? | A traceable problem–evidence–hypothesis argument; closest-work comparison; limits of novelty; counterarguments and what would change the interpretation. |
| **Implementable** | Is there a credible route to carry out this plan with the stated resources? | Essential implementation or derivation details; data and tool access; compute, cost, time, skills and dependencies; early checks and feasible fallbacks. Separate confirmed resources from assumptions. |
| **Measurable** | Does the evaluation measure the claimed concept and distinguish meaningful outcomes and alternatives? | Concept → operational measure or proof → validity and scope; baselines, controls and fair conditions; meaningful bounds and required precision; decisions for beneficial, adverse, negligible/equivalent, and inconclusive outcomes. A proxy benchmark supports only the warranted claim. |

Timely is not a popularity contest: an old but unresolved problem can qualify. Practical includes scientific usefulness. Implementable assesses the documented route, not an unobserved person's ability. Measurable assesses a discriminating plan, not whether the favored result has already occurred; accept appropriate theoretical or qualitative evidence rather than forcing every field into a benchmark.

## Anchor scores before assigning them

Use integer **0–4** scores with equal weight by default. Apply these anchors to each criterion using the evidence above:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0** | The supplied, in-scope proposal omits the essential connection or contains a demonstrated contradiction that defeats this criterion. Name the omission or contradiction. |
| **1** | The proposal asserts the connection, but supporting reasoning or a consequential design choice remains largely unspecified. |
| **2** | A concrete route and some support exist, but a material gap could change the judgment. Identify it. |
| **3** | The connection is coherent and supported within the stated scope; consequential choices are specified and remaining assumptions have credible checks. |
| **4** | The connection also withstands the strongest relevant counterargument or constraint, with explicit boundaries and informative alternatives. This does not require executing the research. |

**Unassessed is not zero.** Use `미평가 / unassessed` when the supplied material or access is insufficient to judge. Distinguish an omitted evaluation in a supplied full proposal from an evaluation section outside a supplied abstract. Distinguish a documented unsupported claim from evidence that exists but cannot be inspected. Explain the missing input. If enough evidence remains for a bounded judgment, score that scope and state the limitation.

Do not automatically reward recent citations, long bibliographies, fluent prose, expensive experiments, or elaborate figures. Do not average subcriteria mechanically. Explain borderline judgments rather than inventing decimal precision.

## Keep the researcher distinct from the document

The default five scores assess the **proposal and its documented plan**. An AI-written explanation is not evidence that the researcher can explain or implement it. If asked about the person's understanding or capability, report that separately as unassessed unless actual explanations or relevant work are available.

When understanding is requested, ask the researcher to explain without specialist terms what they want to learn or change, why current approaches fall short, who benefits, and what first observation would change the plan. Use their actual answer to assess understanding; an AI-written answer on their behalf is not a demonstration. Do not block ordinary document assessment on an unsolicited interview.

## Produce the scorecard and the next decision

State the proposal revision, assessment date, inspected inputs, survey cutoff and coverage, and whether this is self-review or a separate review. If no survey was available, say so; verify a consequential source when tools and scope permit, but never fabricate a current trend check.

When a publication target matters, use the declared audience and contribution type as context. Read [venue-fit guidance](venue-fit.md) for the separate target judgment; retain the declared score anchors and weights unless the user requests a different rubric. Record target/year/track or article type with the assessment. A venue change is not itself a research-quality improvement, and unknown venue rules must not silently become a scientific penalty or an invented acceptance estimate.

| Criterion | Score / 4 or unassessed | Proposal / source location and reasoning | Main gap and next useful action |
|---|---|---|---|

For **all five assessed**, report `sum / 20`; optionally also `sum × 5 / 100`. Explain that the total is a coarse prioritization aid. If **any criterion is unassessed**, report `k / 5 criteria assessed` and the per-criterion scores; withhold a full total and do not rescale the known subset to 100. A requested alternative aggregate must explicitly disclose missingness and must not masquerade as a complete score.

Always report separately:

- **Unresolved core defects**, their locations and consequences, regardless of the total. No score establishes readiness by itself. A discriminating test missing at the center of the proposal remains a blocker even if the other four scores are high.
- **Manageable assumptions** with verification steps, distinguished from those defects.
- **The highest-priority improvements**, ordered by research consequence, not the easiest way to gain points.
- **Researcher understanding / capability status** when relevant; never infer it from the document score.

Do not invent pass thresholds. Do not claim that a score of 80 predicts acceptance, novelty, reproducibility, or eventual effectiveness. A well-designed test of an uncertain hypothesis can score highly while remaining scientifically unproven.

When comparing revisions, use the same rubric, scope, and evidence access. Attribute score changes to actual evidence or design changes; do not claim improvement from rephrasing alone. Do not estimate the score after a suggested fix as if the fix were already made. Review-only requests return the assessment without rewriting the proposal or running experiments.
