# Proposal completion criteria

Use these criteria to assess a prospective research plan. Follow the user's discipline, template, length, and requested scope.

When a publication target is specified, read [publication-target guidance](publication-target.md). Connect the audience, contribution type, verified criteria, required evidence, and available resources. Keep scientific readiness separate from venue fit, and the proposal's requested format separate from an eventual submission's format. An unspecified target does not block a defensible proposal.

## The reasoning chain

**Important problem → inspected evidence → concrete question → testable hypothesis → feasible method → discriminating evaluation → outcome-dependent decisions → resources and alternatives**

These need not be separate chapters. A short concept note can show the essential connections; a detailed protocol needs greater specificity.

## Nine questions

1. **Problem and importance:** Who needs what change or knowledge, under which conditions, and what evidence supports the need? Can a reader understand the motivation without specialist terms?
2. **Prior work and difference:** What is known, which work is closest, and what difference will be tested? Where is novelty still unverified?
3. **Question and hypothesis:** What will be learned, and what observation would weaken or revise the hypothesis?
4. **Method:** What are the inputs, outputs, information requirements, mechanism, assumptions, and reasons for choices? Must a reader invent any essential design decision?
5. **Evaluation:** What are the baselines, data, metrics, budgets, and selection rules? Does each measure capture the claimed concept, within what limits, and how will alternatives be distinguished?
6. **Interpretation:** Before results, what meaningful bounds and precision separate benefit, harm, negligible/equivalent effects, and inconclusive evidence? Which design assumptions support a causal claim, and which alternatives remain?
7. **Feasibility:** What supports the data, equipment, staffing, cost, and time assumptions, including applicable permissions? Which critical assumption gets the first inexpensive informative check, and what decision follows?
8. **Risks and alternatives:** What are the main failure modes, early checks, and scope-reduction or pivot conditions?
9. **Consistency and traceability:** Do claims, citations, variables, terminology, methods, evaluation, resources, and schedules agree?

The story should let the intended reader follow **need → limits of current knowledge → question → proposed insight → required evidence → conditional contribution**. Motivation must be evidenced, prior work represented fairly, and a prospective contribution must remain conditional on the results. See the [argument and feasibility table](research-argument.md) for a compact working format.

## Defects versus uncertainty

- **Core defect:** An undefined question, unsupported factual claim, missing essential method, nondiscriminating evaluation, or infeasible plan with no alternative. Resolve it or adjust the claim or scope.
- **Manageable assumption:** An effect to be investigated, throughput to estimate with a pilot, or access pending with a viable fallback. State its status, verification plan, and consequence if false.
- **Optional improvement:** Clearer wording, additional visualization, or an extension unnecessary for the current claim. Do not postpone a reviewable plan indefinitely for optional polish.

A draft can be written with explicit assumptions. Report unresolved core defects separately from manageable assumptions. An aggregate score must not conceal a decisive defect.

The plan should support five judgments: **Timely** (why now), **Practical** (useful practical or scientific knowledge), **Analytical** (explainable reasoning and alternatives), **Implementable** (a credible resource-bounded route), and **Measurable** (a test capable of distinguishing outcomes). Its literature survey should support these judgments with a synthesis of closest, recent, foundational, and contrary work, bounded by actual search coverage. A document can demonstrate a clear argument without establishing the researcher's personal understanding. Numerical scoring is optional and does not replace these completion criteria.

## Review after writing

Check the evidence and core connections through an actual self-review or separately requested review. Apply concrete corrections and recheck affected sections. Record only reviewers, tools, and runs that actually occurred.

For a visual proposal or poster, the reasoning chain must also survive compression: classifications, labels, arrows, and planned-versus-observed status must agree with the prose. A requested one-page artifact should be rendered and checked for legibility, source access, and page count; attractive layout does not resolve a scientific defect.

If a question cannot be resolved with available evidence, identify the missing input and why it matters. Deliver the current revision and remaining work when further rewording cannot create the needed evidence.

When the purpose is to seek feedback, assess whether the current question, evidence, and unresolved choices are clear enough for a useful discussion. A brief may be ready for that discussion even with a core defect; do not describe it as scientifically complete or ready to execute. Identify the feedback needed to resolve the defect and the decision it affects. Ordinary proposal completion still requires the reasoning chain above.
