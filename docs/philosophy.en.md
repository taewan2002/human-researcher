# The philosophy of Human Researcher

[한국어](philosophy.md) · [README](../README.en.md)

Human Researcher makes the researcher's reasoning easier to inspect: what matters, what is supported, what is uncertain, and what should happen next.

## A proposal you can defend

The goal is a complete, defensible proposal. The research result may still be unknown; the plan should explain how to investigate it.

Every part should connect: the importance of the problem, the closest prior work, the question, the mechanism, the evaluation, the interpretation of outcomes, and the available resources. Distinguish a missing essential connection from a reasonable assumption with a verification plan.

Draft, inspect, revise, and recheck the affected connections. Stop when the scoped core defects are resolved, or clearly identify the external input still needed. Document length and endless polishing do not establish completeness.

## Learn through feedback

A useful milestone explains which uncertainty it addresses and what decision its evidence could change. Reading, implementation, and experiments support that learning; their volume alone does not establish progress.

When preparing for a research meeting, expose the question, current evidence, unresolved choices, and specific feedback needed. A draft with a core defect can be ready to discuss that defect without being ready to execute. Preserve the researcher's choices, distinguish advice from evidence, and record only feedback actually received. Meeting preparation is optional and does not add approval gates to ordinary proposal work.

We refined these practices with reference to public excerpts from the two volumes of 『대학원생 때 알았더라면 좋았을 것들』. The [source notes (Korean)](research-foundations.md) distinguish the books' advice from our operational adaptations and document the inspected pages. They do not attribute every existing project principle to the books or imply author endorsement.

## How we read and explore

**Read top-down.** Establish the problem, main claim, and evidence before deciding which methods, equations, or appendices deserve close reading.

**Follow what came next.** Prioritize papers that actually cite the seed paper. Track what changed or was challenged. Earlier references remain useful for necessary background.

**Follow questions across people and labs.** Author and lab trajectories can reveal how a problem develops. Verify identities, citation relationships, and time-specific affiliations.

**Keep a way back to the source.** Retain versions, access scope, and section, page, figure, or table anchors. A useful summary allows the researcher to inspect its basis.

**Synthesize the literature.** Foundations, recent developments, closest alternatives, and contrary findings should explain what is known and what changes the proposed question. A long bibliography is not a survey, and a survey without an explicit search scope is not evidence of exhaustive coverage.

## How we develop a plan

**Protect the difference between evidence and expectation.** A paper's reported result, an independently observed result, an interpretation, and a hypothesis have different status.

**Keep the strongest alternative explanation.** An idea improves when the design can distinguish it from a simpler account. Complexity alone neither establishes value nor disproves it.

**Find the smallest informative test.** Each comparison should support a decision. More runs are not automatically better research.

**Preserve the researcher's judgment.** Keep their chosen direction and existing text. When a decision is delegated, proceed with reasons. Do not invent approval steps or record an AI recommendation as a human decision.

**Let evidence change the plan.** Preserve contrary findings and explain changes to claims or decisions. Raw sources and observations remain distinct from derived notes.

## Estimate the work and specify interpretations early

Assess the need, tractability, required evidence, resources, and risks together. Test the hardest assumption with the smallest informative check. Estimate person time, device time, and external waiting separately, with continue/narrow/stop conditions. Include data rights, consent, and institutional review when they are actual dependencies of the proposed study.

Specify meaningful bounds and required precision before observing results. Distinguish benefit, harm, evidence of a negligible effect, and insufficient precision. Nonsignificance alone is not equivalence; adequate equivalence evidence supports a conclusion within its bounds. A disappearing adjusted association alone does not identify a mechanism, while an appropriate causal design may support a scoped inference.

Check that the operational measure captures the claimed concept. A benchmark result does not automatically establish general ability; a question explicitly limited to that benchmark need not prove a broader claim it does not make.

## Build a story the reader can inspect

Connect an evidenced need, a fair account of current approaches, the unresolved question, proposed insight, required evidence, and conditional contribution. Do not invent the ending or erase contrary evidence to make the story persuasive.

When asked to assess understanding, invite the researcher to explain the purpose, current limitations, value, and decision criteria without jargon. An AI-written answer is not evidence of the person's understanding. Ordinary drafting does not require an interview.

## Know whom the contribution is for

A research purpose is more specific than “publish a paper.” Name the audience, the question it needs answered, and the contribution: a method, explanation, theory, measurement, replication, dataset, system, or synthesis. A publication target helps turn that purpose into a concrete evidence plan.

Use the exact conference edition and track or journal article type. Verify applicable official criteria; distinguish them from patterns in published papers and the agent's recommendations. Do not alter the evidence or invent novelty to fit a fashionable venue. If a target and the researcher's resources conflict, explain the choices rather than silently changing the question.

The research can be valuable yet unsuitable for a particular track. Topic fit does not establish scientific readiness, and neither predicts acceptance. An internal proposal should remain useful before a venue is chosen.

## Assess to improve the plan

Five questions guide a requested proposal assessment: **Timely**, why now; **Practical**, what useful practical or scientific knowledge; **Analytical**, how the reasoning withstands alternatives; **Implementable**, how to carry it out under real constraints; and **Measurable**, how outcomes can discriminate among explanations.

Scores need evidence, limitations, and useful next actions. They are coarse judgments for improving a plan, not a validated scale or a prediction of success. Missing access is different from a demonstrated omission, and a strong total does not erase a core defect. A prospective study can have an excellent evaluation plan before producing a single result.

Human Researcher keeps document quality separate from the human's understanding. An agent can help write an explanation, but only the researcher's own explanation or relevant work supports a judgment about their ability. The goal remains a proposal the researcher can defend.

## Make reasoning visible

A taxonomy is an argument about how a field divides into families of approaches. Its branches need definitions and evidence; a hybrid can connect to several families, and an unknown property should not be forced into a convenient branch. Category nodes and representative methods have different roles. Locating an idea in the hierarchy helps explain it, but a new branch does not establish novelty.

A method diagram should expose the proposed change. An evaluation diagram should explain what observation would distinguish the hypothesis from its strongest alternative. A one-page poster should let another researcher follow that chain without pretending unresolved choices have been settled.

Use hierarchy, space, labels, and diagrams to reveal connections. Preserve sources and uncertainty when compressing prose into an infographic. Attractive presentation cannot substitute for evidence. The visual and the written proposal should remain consistent as the plan changes.

## Seven research skills and one optional guide

Reading, tracing, mapping, developing, designing, writing, and reviewing are recognizable user goals. Novelty checks, counterarguments, experiments, schedules, and revisions belong inside those tasks.

`using-human-researcher` helps choose a next task when the path is unclear. It is not a mandatory gateway and does not assume other packages are installed.

Each research skill is independently usable. The researcher should not have to manage a catalog of tiny internal steps. A short summary should remain a short summary.

Version 0.1 focuses on proposal development. Autonomous experiments, completed manuscript writing, and reviewer correspondence are outside its packaged scope.

## Open, portable, inspectable

The instructions can run in the user's existing agent with the tools actually available. There is no required Human Researcher service or proprietary database.

Missing access is reported honestly. The project does not equate verified formatting with sound science or an engaging example with measured research usefulness. Its principles should be tested and refined through real, shareable tasks.
